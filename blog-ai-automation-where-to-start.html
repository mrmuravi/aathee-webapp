Where to start with AI automation, without the hype

August 22, 2026 · 4 min read

Most of the teams we talk to don't need an "AI strategy." They need one slow, manual, well-understood process taken off someone's plate. Starting there — small, specific, measurable — beats any grand transformation roadmap.

Look for repetition, not complexity

The best first automation candidate is rarely your hardest problem. It's the task that's repetitive, rule-based, and already well documented in someone's head, even if it's never been written down. Invoice matching, ticket triage, document extraction, and report assembly are common starting points precisely because the rules already exist — they just live in a person, not a system.

Measure the baseline before you build anything

You can't show impact without knowing the starting point. Time the current process, count its error rate, and note how often it needs a human to intervene mid-way. This baseline becomes the only honest way to judge whether automation actually helped.

Design for the exception, not just the happy path

Automation that only handles the easy 80% of cases and silently mishandles the rest erodes trust fast. Build a clear path for cases the system isn't confident about — a queue for human review, not a guess dressed up as a result.

Keep a human in the loop where judgment matters

Full autonomy is rarely the goal, and it's rarely necessary to get most of the value. A system that drafts, flags, and recommends — leaving a person to approve — often delivers most of the time savings with a fraction of the risk.

Ship one process before you plan the next ten

A single automation running reliably in production teaches you more about your data, your edge cases, and your team's appetite for change than any planning exercise. Use that first result to decide what comes next, rather than deciding everything up front.

None of this requires exotic tooling. It requires picking the right first problem and being honest about what "working" means before you start. That's the conversation we have with every client before any automation gets built.
